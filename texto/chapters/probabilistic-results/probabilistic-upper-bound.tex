In this section, we show that one forward pass of constant-depth, polynomial-embedding-dimension, and constant-precision probabilistic transformers can be simulated by shallow Boolean circuits.

\begin{theorem}\label{theo:ac0-sim-prob-transf}
    One step of a probabilistic, constant-depth, polynomial-embedding-dimension transformer can be simulated with a circuit in $\BPAC^0[1]$.
\end{theorem}

For this proof, we build upon \citet{toyota}'s proof of Theorem \ref{theo:ac0-sim-det-transf}.
They already showed that the distribution generator can be simulated by $\AC^0$ circuits.
Thus, we only show that the probabilistic token selector can be simulated by $\BPAC^0[1]$ circuits.

\begin{lemma}
    There is a $\BPAC^0[1]$ circuit that simulates the sampling of a probability distribution for the tokens defined in $\Floating$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proofsketch}\footnote{See Appendix \ref{appendix:sample-bpac} for a complete proof.}
    We define $|\Sigma|$ ranges in $[0,1)$, one per token, each of length equal to the token's probability.
    These ranges can be computed by an $\AC^0$ circuit.
    Then, we use a seed of constant size to index the ranges.
    The seed is generated by the random gates and the ranges are indexable with an $\AC^0$ circuit.
    Thus, the circuit outputs the token $\sigma$ with probability $p(\sigma)$.
\end{proofsketch}

\bigskip

By Theorem \ref{const-random-in-ac0}, one step of a probabilistic, constant-depth, polynomial-embedding-dimension transformer can also be simulated with a circuit in $\AC^0$.
And as i Theorem \ref{theo:det-upper-bound-log-steps}, we now prove that a logarithmic number of $\CoT$ steps is not sufficient to go beyond $\AC^0$, even in a probabilistic transformer.

\begin{theorem}\label{theo:prob-upper-bound-log-steps}
    $\RCoT[\log(n), \poly(n)] \subseteq \BPAC^0[\log(n)] \subset \AC^0$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Since we need a constant number of bits from the seed to simulate each $\CoT$ step, we need $O(\log(n))$ random bits to simulate $\log(n)$ steps.
    Thus, we can enumerate all possible chain-of-thought token sequences $(|\Sigma|^{O(log(n))} = O(n))$ and seeds $2^{O(\log(n)) = O(n)}$ and check when they are consistent.
    Observe that we need to check each seed with each sequence; thus, we check consistency of $O(n^2)$ pairs (sequence, seed).
    
    In the deterministic transformers it was enough to perform an $\OR$ of all the consistent sequences.
    In the probabilistic setting, that idea does not work, since there could be false positives.
    Instead, our circuit computes $\MAJORITY$ of the consistent runs and outputs its result.

    The circuit is clearly in $\AC^0$, except for the $\MAJORITY$.
    The $\MAJORITY$ problem can be solved in $\AC^0$ only when there is a promise that more than a fraction $c$ of the bits are $1$s (or $0$s), for a fixed $c \in (1/2, 1]$ (\citet{viola2009approximate}).
    Since the probabilistic transformer outputs the correct answer $2/3$ of the time, i.e., for $2/3$ of the seeds, we know that at least $2/3$ of the consistent runs agree.
    Thus, instead of $\MAJORITY$, we compute $\MAJORITY_{2/3}$, which can be done in $\AC^0$.

    Finally, since there is an $\AC^0$ circuit simulating one forward pass of constant-depth, polynomial-embedding-dimension, and constant-precision probabilistic transformers, there is an $\AC^0$ circuit simulating $O(\log(n))$ steps of $\CoT$ of the same set-up.
\end{proof}


\bigskip

Since one forward pass of constant-depth, polynomial-embedding-dimension, and constant-precision probabilistic transformers can be simulated by a circuit in $\BPAC^0[1]$, stacking the circuits of $\BPAC^0[1]$ for each $\CoT$ step gives a simulation for $\poly(n)$ steps (as in Corollary \ref{det-poly-steps-in-ppoly}).
This circuit has polynomial size and uses a polynomial number of random bits; thus, we obtain Corollary \ref{prob-poly-steps-in-rppoly}.


\begin{corollary}\label{prob-poly-steps-in-rppoly}
    $\RCoT[\poly(n), \poly(n)] \subseteq \BPppoly$ 
\end{corollary}
