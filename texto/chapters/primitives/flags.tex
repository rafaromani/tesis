For complex operations inside the loop of the transformer we will need to identify when a vector comes from a certain token or position.
In this section we present how to mark the vectors with flags during the embedding.

\bigskip


For flagging a token we use the token embedding, and in the same way, for flagging a position we use the position encoding.
Either way, the strategy is exactly the same.
The embedding dimension is extended to allocate the flags.
A new coordinate is added per flag.
Therefore, using a constant number of flags only adds a constant to the embedding dimension.
Thus, if the flagging is made in a family of transformers its $\CoTtndn$ class does not change.

For example, let $v_\sigma$ and $v_\tau$ be vectors of a transformer $\TF$ where $v_\sigma$ comes from embedding the token $\sigma$ and $v_\tau$ from the token $\tau$ (with $\sigma \neq \tau$).
We can define a transformer $\TF'$ that behaves like $\TF$ but recognize when a vector comes from embedding the token $\sigma$.
$\TF'$ has embedding dimension $d' = d + 1$ where $d$ is the embedding dimension of $\TF$.

\begin{figure*}[h]
    \begin{align*}
        & v_\sigma = \left(\begin{matrix} x \\ y \\ z \\ \end{matrix}\right) \to v'_\sigma = \left(\begin{matrix} x \\ y \\ z \\ f_{\ON} \end{matrix}\right)
        & v_\tau   = \left(\begin{matrix} i \\ j \\ k \\ \end{matrix}\right) \to v'_\tau   = \left(\begin{matrix} i \\ j \\ k \\ f_{\OFF} \end{matrix}\right)
    \end{align*}
    \caption{Change in a flagged and unflagged vector}
\end{figure*}

For the explicit construction read Appendix \ref{appendix:flags}.

\bigskip

This primitive works also as a mechanism to extend the embedding dimension without changing the behavior.
If $f_{\ON}$ and $f_{\OFF}$ are defined as $0$, the resulting transformer is the same as the initial with an extra coordinate that is always $0$.
