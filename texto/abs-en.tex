\chapter*{\runtitle}

\noindent
Transformers are the basic building block of large language models (LLMs).
In this thesis, we study the formal languages recognized by different theoretical abstractions of transformers when limiting the number of steps in the chain of thought.
First, we build on the work of \citet{toyota} and study the properties of their formal abstraction.
Second, we extend their model to incorporate the stochastic behavior of transformers, which has received little attention in the literature.
We study the formal languages recognizable by our definition of probabilistic transformers when limiting the number of steps in the chain of thought.
Our analysis is carried out through the lens of Boolean circuit complexity.
Finally, we relate both models to Turing machines and provide arguments regarding when the randomness of transformers can and cannot be ignored in order to study their expressive power.

Our main results yield that transformers doing $T(n)$ steps of chain of thought are equivalent to Boolean circuits of size $T(n)$.
This holds true for deterministic and probabilistic transformers and circuits, even in the $\Ptime$-uniform or nonuniform setting.

\bigskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} probabilistic transformers, chain-of-thought, Boolean circuits, formal languages.
